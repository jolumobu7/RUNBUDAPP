Child safety standards

RunBud (by JOLU, myrunbud.com) · Last updated November 26, 2026

RunBud has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE), including child sexual abuse material (CSAM), grooming, sextortion and trafficking. Any account involved is removed permanently, the content is preserved for law enforcement, and we report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) and to law enforcement as the law requires.

Who uses RunBud

RunBud is for athletes 13 and older and the coaches, athletic trainers, nutritionists and school administrators who work with them. Many athletes are 13–17, so the app is built on the assumption that minors are present.

What is prohibited

  • Any sexual content involving minors, or content that sexualizes minors in any way.
  • Grooming: an adult building a relationship with a minor for sexual purposes, including asking for photos, moving the conversation off RunBud, or asking a minor to keep things secret.
  • Sextortion, threats, or requests for images.
  • Sharing phone numbers, emails or social media handles to contact minors privately (the app blocks phone numbers and emails in messages and the forum).

How the app is designed to protect minors

  • No hidden adult-to-athlete chats. Any conversation that includes a coach or other staff member is visible to every staff member on that team.
  • School oversight. Administrators of a school or club can read all messages on their linked teams, including athlete-to-athlete.
  • Messages can’t be edited or deleted, so there is always a record.
  • No photos or videos can be sent in messages or the forum.
  • No anonymous or random chat: people can only message others on a team they joined with a code from their coach.
  • Everyone agrees to community rules before using the forum, and everyone sees a safety notice before messaging.

How to report

  • In the app: long-press any message (or tap the flag on a forum post) and choose Report. You can also block the person.
  • By email: support@myrunbud.com (subject “Child safety report”).
  • If a child is in immediate danger, call 911 (in the US) first. You can also report to NCMEC’s CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org.

Reports are reviewed within 24 hours. Content that breaks these standards is hidden right away, the accounts involved are banned, and the record is kept for law enforcement.
